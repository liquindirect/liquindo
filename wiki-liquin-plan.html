Liquin's Plan (of Action), abbreviated L'PA, is described as the founding and current governing administration of Liquindo.

It describes itself as an administrative structure rather than a conventional political party and is associated with a centralized, meritocratic, authoritarian, and autocratic model of government.

History

Liquin's Plan was formally established in late 2024 following the earlier formation of the Liquindo Nationalist Front (LNF) on 16 December 2023.

The LNF initially operated as a nationalist political movement, while Liquin's Plan presented itself as the formal administrative structure through which Liquindo would be developed and governed.

The LNF was later incorporated into Liquin's Plan as an internal faction rather than continuing as a separate rival organization.

Liquin's Plan has also historically been opposed to Vaughtonology, which developed as a competing political tendency within the wider history of Liquindo.

Vexillology

The administration's flag incorporates the navy and mustard yin-yang symbolism also used in Liquindo's national imagery.

In this variant, the smaller contrasting circles of the yin-yang are replaced by additional yin-yang designs.

Demographic Objectives

True Liquindians

One long-term objective associated with Liquin's Plan is the development of a stable native-born population described within the project as “True Liquindians”.

Proposals connected with this objective have included policies intended to increase the birth rate and encourage long-term cultural integration.

Earlier proposals also described highly controlled immigration, citizenship, and family-policy systems. Such ideas have raised ethical concerns because state-directed demographic policies can interfere with individual rights and personal autonomy.

Genetic-health proposals

Some earlier policy documents proposed genetic-health screening for prospective parents with the stated aim of reducing hereditary disease.

These proposals have been controversial because coercive or discriminatory reproductive policies raise serious medical, ethical, and human-rights concerns.

Cultural Objectives

Return to Cellular Communication

Liquin's Plan has proposed reducing dependence on smartphones and internet-based communication services outside the home.

Under this concept, simpler mobile phones would primarily be used for calls, while personal computers would remain the main way households access digital services.

Social Policy

Liquin's Plan supports a socially conservative framework based on its interpretation of traditional family and moral values.

Its proposals have included restrictions on pornography, recreational drugs, same-sex marriage, adoption policy, and some gender-transition practices.

These positions are politically and socially contested and have been criticized on grounds including individual liberty, discrimination, and equal treatment.

Migrant Assessment Operation

A proposed migrant-assessment system would evaluate applicants using factors such as criminal history, financial circumstances, and integration requirements.

Critics of such systems argue that migration policy requires clear legal safeguards to avoid discriminatory treatment and violations of due-process rights.

Economic Objectives

Work-Based Housing Allocation System

The Work-Based Housing Allocation System (WBHAS) is a proposed state-managed housing model in which housing would be allocated according to a Housing Value Score (HVS).

The score has been described as taking account of factors such as work, community contribution, and other civic measures.

Higher scores would provide access to more desirable housing, while lower scores could result in reassignment to other accommodation.

Supporters describe the model as a way of allocating scarce housing according to contribution and public need. Critics argue that a system linking housing to political or behavioural compliance could become coercive or discriminatory.

Factions

Liquin's Plan contains several internal political factions. These organizations are presented as different interpretations of the wider state project rather than as independent competing political parties.

Factions may disagree on particular policies but remain subordinate to the wider administrative structure.
