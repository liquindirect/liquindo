Liquindo, officially the Federal Republic of Liquindo, is envisioned as a crowdfunded island nation; a refuge from global instability, an experimental society blending modern innovation, traditional values, and self-sufficiency.

Etymology

Liquindo is a play on the Portuguese words "Lindo" and "Liquido", translating to "Beautiful" and "Liquid", the latter referring to water.

History

Liquindo's primary conception was thought of in 2021 when Diego Liquin was still in secondary school. The idea was inspired by the Netflix documentary film Rose Island.

Since 2021, there have been multiple revivals of Liquindo, most notably from Finn Vaughton (2023) and Edward Fullwood (2025). The latter is ongoing. The first physical Liquindo flag ever created was made on the 3rd of July 2025, and other merchandise has been created such as pin-badges and stickers.

Development of the Liquindian language started on the 16th of November 2025. It currently has 200+ words.

Vexillology

Liquindo has undergone three flag changes since its establishment in 2021 but maintains its current flag: a yellow, navy, and white tricolor with a yin-yang charge rotated 90 degrees counter-clockwise and recolored to navy and yellow. It also hosts a star in the upper-middle of the yin-yang. The star signifies authority, navy represents integrity, mustard represents the people, and white represents faith.

Government

As Liquindo's development has not started, such as buying the island, the following information is theoretical.

Liquindo would operate as a junta, led by Diego Liquin and below him his investors who would sway and appeal decisions.

Pre-Democratization

In a time pre-democratization, Liquindo would operate as an elective autocracy. The first dictator (Diego Liquin) would appoint 5 political advisors. After his death, these advisors would elect a new dictator. The new dictator would then appoint 5 new advisors, allowing the cycle to repeat itself. The dictator's powers would be bound by his advisors, and since he appointed them himself, there would be no political struggle unless the advisors truly doubt the dictator's ability to rule.

Permanent Structure

Liquindo would be an autocratic regime ruled under the administration of Liquin's Plan. The director would serve as head of state. Instead of democracy, parties would be assimilated into the administration, under the approval of Liquin and those already accepted, and converted into factions, which the public can join. This would avoid any severe political interference in contrast to the administration's ideologies.

Military

The military in Liquindo is not a separate warrior caste or professional standing army, but a permanent national militia integrated directly into society. Every citizen is expected to participate in national defense through regular training, discipline, and readiness, making military service part of ordinary civic life rather than a specialized career. The nation is therefore understood as an armed community capable of collective defense at all times. Citizens retrain periodically throughout their lives, creating a society where preparedness, endurance, and sacrifice become normal cultural expectations rather than exceptional wartime measures.

The military exists primarily for defense and deterrence rather than conquest or ideological expansion. War is viewed as a tragic necessity rather than a glorious pursuit, and the ideal outcome is preventing invasion or collapse through overwhelming readiness and national unity. Because of this defensive philosophy, the armed forces are designed around resilience, mobilization, territorial familiarity, and social cohesion instead of permanent foreign deployment.

The state seeks to create a population that is difficult to intimidate, occupy, or destabilize because the military structure is inseparable from the civilian population itself.

Military culture is intentionally spartan and anti-decadent. Rank and authority are earned through merit, sacrifice, and combat performance rather than wealth or aristocratic inheritance. Officers are expected to embody discipline, calmness, and personal endurance, and leadership positions throughout the state require military experience to ensure that political authority is connected to service and sacrifice.

The ideal soldier is not fanatical or reckless but composed, loyal, spiritually grounded, and prepared to suffer for the survival of the nation if necessary.

Although there is no massive professional standing army, elite guard formations and intelligence institutions can still exist to protect the leadership, monitor threats, and coordinate national security. Intelligence and special operations are considered important because the state sees modern instability, foreign influence, and internal decay as ongoing dangers even during peacetime.

However, these institutions remain subordinate to the national militia structure rather than replacing it.

The military also performs major civilian functions. During disasters, infrastructure crises, or national emergencies, militia units assist with relief, logistics, reconstruction, and public order. This reinforces the idea that the military is not merely an instrument of violence but a guardian of national continuity and survival.

Environmental protection, self-sufficiency, and civil defense all become part of military planning, with strategic reserves and domestic production treated as essential to long-term resilience.

Aesthetically and culturally, the military favors traditionalism over futuristic spectacle. Uniforms, ceremonies, and symbols emphasize continuity, simplicity, and collective identity rather than individual expression. Weapons ownership is regulated and tied directly to militia participation, meaning arms are viewed as instruments of duty rather than personal freedom.

Overall, the military functions as the disciplined backbone of society: calm, defensive, sacrificial, and permanently intertwined with the nation itself.

Economy

Liquindo's economy is built around the idea that economic life should serve the nation, the family, and social stability rather than individual profit or consumerism. Heavy industry, infrastructure, banking, energy, and other strategic sectors are owned and directed by the state, which uses them to maintain national self-sufficiency, military preparedness, and long-term prosperity.

Industrial production is planned according to national needs rather than market competition, and large private corporations do not exist. The economy is therefore highly centralized in industry, with the state acting as the organizer and coordinator of production.

At the same time, agriculture is decentralized and hereditary. Farms are owned by families and remain within family dynasties across generations. Land cannot be freely expanded, traded, or accumulated into large estates, preventing the rise of landlords or agrarian monopolies.

Rural life is treated not only as an economic necessity but also as the cultural and spiritual foundation of the nation. The countryside preserves continuity, tradition, and social cohesion, while also guaranteeing food security and limiting dependence on foreign imports.

Markets exist only in a very limited form. The economy does not prioritize free competition, speculative finance, luxury consumption, or constant growth for its own sake. Prices and production are largely directed by the state according to perceived social usefulness and national priorities.

Innovation and entrepreneurship are allowed only when approved by the state and when they benefit the collective good rather than private accumulation. Foreign corporations and excessive international trade are rejected in favor of near-total autarky, with the nation attempting to produce as much as possible domestically.

Work is viewed primarily as a duty and necessity rather than a path to personal enrichment. Citizens are expected to contribute productively to society, either through agriculture, industry, military service, or other forms of labor.

Strikes are illegal because labor conflict is considered harmful to national unity, and the state coordinates labor relations directly. Wealth inequality is minimized by preventing large concentrations of private capital, although exceptional service and merit can still be rewarded by the state.

The ideal economy is therefore disciplined, productive, self-sufficient, environmentally conscious, and socially unified, rejecting both global capitalism and fully collectivized communism in favor of a nationally directed communitarian system.

Political Factions

Factions are represented by the populace, and hence Liquindo is a one-party state. Political parties are replaced by factions. However, only selected factions are accepted if they align with Liquindo's current ideology. So factions can be rejected more often than parties in other nations.
